One-time vs recurring costs

  • One-time or project-based — initial design, custom photography, logo refresh, migration help, or a paid setup fee.
  • Recurring — domain renewal, hosting, SaaS subscriptions, SSL (often included), maintenance retainers, and premium plugins.
  • Hidden recurring: your time — evenings spent editing pages, chasing form spam, or learning a dashboard count as cost even if they never appear on a card statement.

Cost categories owners should line-item

Domain name

Most businesses register a .com or regional domain through a registrar. Retail registration and renewal prices vary by registrar and TLD; check the registrar’s published price at purchase time. Budget roughly tens of dollars per year for a standard domain — not hundreds — unless you buy a premium aftermarket name.

Hosting and infrastructure

Shared hosting for a small WordPress site often falls in a low monthly or annual range at introductory rates; renewal pricing can be higher. Managed WordPress hosts charge more but include updates and backups. All-in-one builders frequently bundle hosting into the subscription — confirm whether a custom domain is included on your tier.

Design and build

Ranges vary widely by market and scope:

  • DIY builder subscription — you pay the platform; your “design cost” is mostly time.
  • Trade-focused subscription — structured product with templates; compare subscription TCO over 1–3 years.
  • Freelancer projects — often quoted as a flat project fee; ask what revisions and launch support include.
  • Agency builds — commonly higher project fees plus optional retainers; verify content, photography, and SEO scope in writing.

We do not publish competitor or agency price lists here because they change and may not apply to your market.

Maintenance and updates

After launch, someone must update hours, add services, refresh license numbers, and fix broken links. Either you do it, your office manager does it, or you pay a provider. WordPress sites especially need plugin and core updates for security.

Optional add-ons

Call tracking numbers, scheduling widgets, live chat, stock photo licenses, email marketing tools, and local SEO services are separate from “having a website.” Include them only if you will actually use them.

What to compare when vendors quote you

  1. Is hosting included, and for how long?
  2. Who owns the domain and how is it registered?
  3. How many revision rounds and who writes plumbing-specific copy?
  4. Are service-area pages included, and will they be unique or duplicated city templates?
  5. What happens if you cancel — can you export content or redirect the domain?
  6. Are phone contact and forms included and tested on mobile?
  7. Is SSL included and automatic?

DIY time cost (often the largest line item)

If you bill your own labor at even a modest internal rate, ten evenings at two hours each can exceed a year of a simple subscription. Be honest about whether you will finish a DIY site or stall at 80%. The how-to build guide helps you scope work; the checklist helps you confirm nothing critical is missing before launch.
