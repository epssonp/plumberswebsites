Use this plumbing website checklist to verify that your site matches your real business before customers depend on it. It complements — does not replace — the full process in How to Build a Plumbing Website, which covers planning, approach selection, and launch workflow in depth.

Checking boxes does not guarantee SEO rankings or a specific number of leads. It reduces embarrassing and costly mistakes: wrong numbers, missing services, and trust claims you cannot support.

How to use this list

  • Essential items should be correct before you publish.
  • Recommended items improve clarity and trust; add them as soon as practical.
  • Optional items depend on budget and capacity — skip rather than fake them.

Planning and content prep

  • Essential: List services you actually offer (and note any you want to decline).
  • Essential: List cities or regions you truly serve — consistent with dispatch reality.
  • Essential: Confirm legal business name, phone, and primary contact email.
  • Recommended: Gather license numbers, insurance statements, and association memberships you are allowed to display.
  • Recommended: Collect 3–5 project photos you have rights to use.
  • Optional: Draft a short “our approach” story (how you treat homes, clean-up, estimates).

Core pages (five-page model)

Many plumbing sites use Home, Services, About, Contact, plus an approach or values page. PlumbersWebsites.com follows that model; other builders may differ — adapt the checklist to your structure.

  • Essential: Home explains who you help and primary services in plain language.
  • Essential: Services page matches what dispatchers say on the phone.
  • Essential: About page identifies ownership or leadership honestly.
  • Essential: Contact page shows phone, hours, and form or email path you monitor.
  • Recommended: Approach or values page sets expectations (estimates, warranties, communication).
  • Optional: FAQ section if answers are stable — avoid duplicate FAQ spam across pages.

Contact and conversion

  • Essential: Phone number is click-to-call on mobile and matches your main business line.
  • Essential: Someone answers or returns form submissions during stated hours.
  • Recommended: Phone appears in header or sticky area on key pages.
  • Recommended: Form fields are minimal (name, contact, brief issue, city).
  • Optional: After-hours message explains whether emergency service exists.

Service areas and local accuracy

  • Essential: Service area copy matches where you send trucks — no fabricated nationwide coverage.
  • Recommended: Mention notable suburbs or counties you serve without duplicating twenty thin city pages.
  • Optional: Map embed if it helps orientation and loads acceptably on mobile.

Trust and compliance

  • Essential: License and insurance claims are current and verifiable.
  • Essential: No fake reviews, awards, or “#1 in city” badges without proof.
  • Recommended: Link to privacy policy and terms if you collect form data (see your platform’s legal page options).
  • Optional: Manufacturer certifications only where your techs hold them.

Technical readiness

  • Essential: Site loads on mobile over cellular data without broken layout.
  • Essential: Custom domain connected (if promised) and HTTPS works without browser warnings.
  • Essential: Favicon and browser title show your business name, not a generic placeholder.
  • Recommended: Test forms — submit a test message and receive it.
  • Recommended: Basic analytics or call tracking if you already use those tools — configure before announcing the new URL.
  • Optional: Structured data where appropriate; do not expect rich results as a guarantee.

SEO basics (without overpromising)

  • Essential: Unique page titles and meta descriptions that describe real pages (not keyword stuffing).
  • Essential: One clear H1 per page aligned with visible content.
  • Recommended: Google Business Profile updated to match NAP (name, address, phone) where applicable.
  • Recommended: Internal links between services and contact paths.
  • Optional: Local content projects (blog, tips) only if you will maintain them.

Launch day

  • Essential: Click every nav link; fix 404s.
  • Essential: Call your own number from the site on a phone.
  • Essential: Confirm published URL is the one you put on trucks, email, and GBP.
  • Recommended: Ask one employee or friend to find your phone number in under five seconds.
  • Optional: Announce launch to existing customers via email or social — only if accurate.

First 30 days after launch

  • Essential: Update seasonal hours or holiday closures on the site.
  • Recommended: Review form spam; adjust fields or captcha if needed.
  • Recommended: Scan for outdated service promises (e.g., discontinued promotions).
  • Optional: Compare inquiry volume to prior baseline — treat small samples cautiously.
